## The problem

Let $\mathbb{N}_0 = \lbrace 0,1,2,\ldots\rbrace$. Find every function $f:\mathbb{N}_0\to\mathbb{N}_0$ for which

$$
xf(y)+yf(x)=(x+y)f(x^2+y^2)
$$

holds for all $x,y\in\mathbb{N}_0$.

Source: [2002 Canadian Mathematical Olympiad, Problem 5](https://w7.cms.math.ca/Competitions/CMO/archive/exam2002.pdf). I found it in *Putnam and Beyond*.

## My write-up

Every constant function $f(n)=c$, with $c\in\mathbb{N}_0$, satisfies the equation. I claim these are the only solutions.

Suppose $f$ is not constant. Setting $x=0$ and $y=1$ gives $f(0)=f(1)$, so there must be positive integers $x,y$ with $f(x)<f(y)$. By well-ordering, choose such a pair for which the positive integer

$$
\Delta=f(y)-f(x)
$$

is as small as possible. Set $z=x^2+y^2$. The given equation says

$$
f(z)=\frac{xf(y)+yf(x)}{x+y}
    =f(x)+\frac{x}{x+y}\Delta.
$$

Since $x,y>0$, we have $0<\frac{x}{x+y}<1$. Hence

$$
f(x)<f(z)<f(y).
$$

But $z$ is a positive integer and $f(z)-f(x)$ is a positive integer smaller than $\Delta$, contradicting our choice of $x,y$. Therefore $f$ is constant, and every solution is $f(n)=c$ for some $c\in\mathbb{N}_0$.
